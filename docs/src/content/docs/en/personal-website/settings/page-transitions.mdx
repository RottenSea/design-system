Two things every page has without a component.

## Page transitions

```astro
---
import "@rottensea/ds-personal-website/page-transitions.css";
---
```

Changing page fades the old page out and the new one in over `--motion-base`, while the nav stays where it is. These are the browser's own cross-document view transitions: every page still loads in full and runs its scripts, and Back and Forward return to where the reader was. A browser without them changes page at once, and so does a reader who asked for reduced motion, in the OS or with SettingsMenu.

The stylesheet is separate so a site chooses it; the docs site does not use it.

## The scrollbar

`base.css` makes the page's scrollbar thin, in the soft muted text colour on the page's own ground, and keeps its space whether the page scrolls or not, so a short page and a long one centre the same. Only the right side is kept: keeping both would leave a strip of canvas beside every full-width dark band, such as the footer. Browsers that ignore `scrollbar-color` and `scrollbar-width` draw their own, and macOS draws overlay scrollbars that take no space.
